Lesson 14: How music influences and challenges ideas

Lesson 14 of 30 · L12 · Music of Australia

  • Bring: Headphones and log

Learning intention

We are learning how music can influence and challenge ideas.

Cue sheet

One work, three moments in its arrangement. Press the name to play it from the top, or a time to drop into that moment.

  1. 0:12 into I Was Only 19The band swells here, then pulls back within a few bars. Listen for the arrangement holding itself in rather than letting the moment grow.
  2. 3:33 into I Was Only 19The fullest and most sustained the arrangement ever gets, and the only point in the song where it stays this way for this long.
  3. 4:15 into I Was Only 19It fades rather than finishing on a chord. A work like this does not get a triumphant last note.

LearnSeparating the argument from the music that carries it

A song can be right about something and still be badly made, and a song can be brilliantly made in service of an argument you disagree with. Judging the two together blurs both judgements, so this lesson asks for them apart.

  • What did the work argue? State the argument itself, in your own words, the way you’d summarise any position, not the song’s mood, its subject matter, or how it made you feel.
  • What musical feature carried it? Name a specific feature — a lyric delivered over a bare texture, a melody that stays plain rather than building, a specific instrumental choice — and say what that feature does to how the argument lands.
  • Connect the two, in that order. The feature only matters because of the effect it has on the argument’s force. Naming a feature without saying what it does to the argument is description; saying what the argument was without naming how the music carries it is a lyric summary, not music analysis.

Music doing work in the world is not a metaphor: a listener’s response to an argument really can change because of how it was set to music rather than only what was said. That working is what this pair of dot points is asking you to make audible.

Worth keeping

Music does work in the world, and the working is audible.
