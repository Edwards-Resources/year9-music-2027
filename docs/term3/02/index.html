LearnThe dot point, now against a marker’s criteria

You already know the shape: a feature, what it sounds like and where, and why it is there. What’s different in an examination response is that a real marker is checking each part against a criteria sheet, not just reading for sense.

What a marker is actually checking

  • The feature has to be named precisely. “The rhythm” is not a feature. “Syncopation in the melody” is.
  • The location has to be specific enough to check. “Later in the piece” cannot be verified against the recording. A bar number, a timing, or a clearly identified section can.
  • The reason has to connect to the feature you named, not restate the general mood of the piece. If your reason would work unchanged for a different feature, it isn’t doing its job.

A precise word in the wrong structure still loses the mark, and a correct structure with vague words loses it just as fast. Both have to be right at once, which is why this lesson marks live: seeing exactly where a real response gains or loses a mark is faster than being told the rule in the abstract.

Worth keeping

Music applies ALARM to build well-formed dot points, not paragraphs.
