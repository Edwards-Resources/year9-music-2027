Lesson 20: Writing the bass and the drum part

Lesson 20 of 30 · C14

LearnWriting for the hands that will actually play it

A walking bass line or a drum part is not finished when it looks right on the page. It’s finished when the specific person playing it, on the instrument they actually have, can play it at tempo without needing three days to learn one bar.

  • Range. A bass guitar’s lowest open string is E, and a line that keeps dropping below it, or climbs so high it runs out of usable string, is not written for the instrument in the room. Check the bottom and the top against what the player can actually reach.
  • Hand position. A line that jumps around the neck constantly is harder to play in tempo than one that mostly stays in one position and moves out of it on purpose. Walking bass, especially, wants mostly stepwise or small-interval movement for exactly this reason.
  • Tempo. A pattern that is playable slowly can become impossible at the tempo the piece is actually meant to go. Test every part at full tempo, not just at the speed you wrote it in.

The check that actually matters is not whether the pattern looks correct against the rules you were given — it’s whether the classmate who has to play it can. They are the reference, not the range chart, because a chart cannot tell you whether a particular stretch is comfortable in their hands.

Worth keeping

A composer writes for the performer, not for the page.
