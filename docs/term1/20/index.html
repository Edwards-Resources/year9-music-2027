Lesson 20: Sharing a recording: copyright, permission and identity

LearnBefore a recording of your group goes anywhere

A rehearsal recording feels private right up until someone wants to post it, and then four separate questions apply, all before anyone presses share.

  • Copyright. The song itself belongs to someone. Performing it in class is one thing; publishing a recording of it publicly is another, and it usually needs a permission you do not have.
  • Permission. Everyone visible or audible in the recording, not just the person posting it, has to agree to being shared. Silence is not agreement.
  • Attribution. If it is shared, the songwriter and original artist are credited properly, not just the band performing it.
  • The school account. A school platform has its own rules about what may be posted from it, separate from copyright and permission, and those rules apply whether or not everyone in the video is fine with it.

None of these checks is optional because the recording “is only going to a few people”. A private chat is still a place a recording can be copied out of, and once it is out, none of these four questions can be undone.

Worth keeping

Copyright and consent are part of performing, not paperwork bolted on afterwards.
