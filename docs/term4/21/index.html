LearnWhere every sound in your project came from

A sound library, a sample pack, and a recording you made yourself carry three different sets of rules about what you’re allowed to do with them, and knowing which one applies to each sound in your project is a compositional responsibility, not paperwork bolted on at the end.

  • Licensed libraries and sample packs usually permit use in coursework, but the licence terms still apply, and a “free” sound is not automatically a sound with no conditions attached.
  • A sample of someone else’s recorded work is a different case again: using an identifiable excerpt of another artist’s recording is closer to using their composition than it is to using a licensed sound effect, and the protocols from Term 1’s sharing lesson apply here too, before the piece is even finished.
  • Something you recorded or performed yourself is the one category where the rights are straightforwardly yours, which is part of why so much of this course is built around composing and playing your own material rather than assembling other people’s.

Auditing your own project means being able to answer, for every single sound in it, where it came from and whether you’re allowed to hand it in under that source. If you can’t answer both for a sound, that is the sound to replace, not the sound to hope nobody asks about.

Worth keeping

Knowing what you may use is part of composing with technology.
