# Lesson depth triage: which of the 123 inputs get book-work content

Built 17 August 2026, before any of it is written, following the Year 10 precedent.
**Nothing on this list is written until the teacher has signed off on the list itself.**

## The test, unchanged from Year 10

Two questions, asked of every one of the 123 inputs:

1. **Does this input introduce something a student writes in their book that currently
   exists only in the teacher's head or in a slide deck?** If yes, one `explain` block of
   about 250 words. One. Nothing else.
2. **Would the teacher stop the recording and talk over it?** If yes, a `guide` cue table.
   Otherwise the `listen` block is enough.

Test 2 is the expensive one and is meant to almost never fire. It fired twice in 28 on
Year 10. It fires four times in 123 here, once per term.

Everything that is practice, rehearsal, performing, workshopping, feedback, reflection,
logging, an assessment sitting or an issue-the-task lesson is left exactly as it is. Those
pages were never broken. A lesson plan is the right artefact for a lesson where the
learning happens in the room with an instrument in your hands.

## Three Year 9 rules the Year 10 triage did not need

**A blueprint sheet is not an explain, and neither replaces the other.** Term 2 declares
four blueprint sheets (`12-bar-progression`, `piano-voicings`, `drum-pattern`,
`walking-bass-rules`) and `PRODUCT.md` promises three more in Term 4 (a timing sheet, a
template, a motif bank). A sheet holds the *what*: the chart, the shapes, the rules, the
pattern, laid out to be read while playing. An explain holds the *why*: how the thing
works and what decision it lets you make. Where this list marks an explain beside a
declared sheet, the two are split on that line and neither repeats the other. Four inputs
were **rejected** on exactly this ground: T2 #12 walking bass, T2 #13 voicings,
T4 #09 the motif bank, T4 #10 shares the timing sheet.

**No explain may name repertoire in the Term 3 Weeks 4 to 6 block.** T3 #11, #12 and #16
carry explains, and all three are written about protocols and about the relationship
between a work and Country in general terms. The consulted selection is not made, and the
two barred works are not named anywhere on the site by design. An explain is prose, which
is the easiest place in the build to leak a title by accident. Watch this line hardest.

**T3 #01 is deliberately refused.** "What Australian music is, and who decides" opens a
question and hands it back in Week 10 to see what moved. An explain there answers the
question the lesson exists to leave open. It is the one input on the list that passes
test 1 on content and is rejected on teaching grounds.

A-only is 28%, so both options are tighter per input than the precedent. Term 3 carries the

## What has to be built before a word is written

**Neither block type exists in Year 9.** `build.py` renders `clauses`, `listen`, `patched`
and the meta row, and nothing else. Both blocks need designing into this world rather than
porting from Year 10, whose zine world is a different system. Two things the world already
decides for us:

- An `explain` is sustained prose, and **long white prose never sits on the black field**.
  So it sits on the sheet, which is what the sheet is for. It is the first block in this
  world that is read rather than done, so it cannot take a clause `kind`.
- A `guide`'s timestamps are counts, and counts in this world are mono, like the input
  numbers on the rail. That grammar already exists.

**The guides need the embedding gap closed first.** A cue table whose times cannot seek a
player is a list of numbers. The four guides sit on inputs T1 #04, T2 #02, T3 #14 and
T4 #02, and every id for those is already verified in the register. So the order is:
embed, then guide.

## Three questions for sign-off

1. **A only, or A and B?** 34 explains and about 8,500 words, or 47 and about 11,750.
   Recommendation: **A only first**, all four terms, then look at the B list against real
   pages rather than against this table.
2. **Does Year 9 get a `worked` block as well?** **Ruled yes on 17 August 2026, and no to
   `check`.** T1 #26, T3 #09 and T4 #19 take a `worked` block instead of a 250-word
   explain: a model answer with the reasoning shown, and a zero-mark answer that looks
   fine and is not. No `check`, since Year 9 has no gapfill for it to answer.
   **Designed, built and documented on 18 August 2026** as the two takes block; see
   `DESIGN.md`. T3 #09 carries the exemplar, T1 #26 and T4 #19 are still to be written.
3. **Confirm the four guides**, or cut to two. Recommendation: **keep four**, one per term,
   but build them after the embedding work rather than alongside it.
