## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML generated by a dependency-free Python build script from per-lesson data files, deployed to GitHub Pages from `main` `/docs`.

Not an open decision. All three sibling sites (`year8-music`, `year10-music-2026`, `year11-music-2027`) are built this way, for a reason first recorded in the Year 10 record and still binding: the teacher writes no code and will not be in a session when something breaks, so a framework's dependency tree is a liability rather than a convenience. No lockfile, no install step, nothing to rot.

Repo name `year9-music-2027`, publishing at `https://edwards-resources.github.io/year9-music-2027/`. **The repo name is load-bearing**: the base path is compiled into every internal link, so renaming the repo breaks the whole site and its Pages URL at once.

## Users

**Primary: the 2027 Year 9 Music elective class**, aged about 14 to 15. This is their **first elective year** and for most of them the first time music has been a subject they chose rather than one they were timetabled into. They work from the site in class on school devices during 60-minute periods, and at home when catching up or finishing a composition.

**This class is the 2026 Year 8 cohort**, who spent all of 2026 inside the Year 8 site's Tour Tee world. They arrive fluent in how these sites work, and they will notice immediately if Year 9 looks like Year 8 with the dates changed. Choosing the elective is the thing that has changed about them, and the site is the first evidence they get that the choice bought them something.

**Secondary: the teacher, teaching from it live.** He projects the page and works down it with the class, in a daylit classroom, with instruments out and hands busy. He does not want to click in and out of Canvas modules mid-lesson to reach a backing track or a chord chart.

## Product Purpose

A live teaching site carrying the Year 9 Music lesson content for all four terms of 2027: what each lesson covers, what to listen to, what to play, what to build, and what the class is expected to be able to do by the end of it. It replaces Canvas content pages entirely.

Success is a full lesson taught from the site without opening anything else, and a student partway through a 16-bar blues finding the walking bass rules and the chord progression on their own without asking twice.

## Positioning

**The scaffold is the subject, and it is designed to shrink.**

The junior sites are lesson-shaped and the senior site is repertoire-shaped. Year 9 is neither. It is the year the student stops being handed everything and starts supplying the musical decision, and the course is explicitly built as a ladder with the support withdrawn one rung at a time:

| Term | What the student is given | What the student supplies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The chart, the chords, the parts | The playing |
| 2 | The progression, the voicings, the drum pattern, the walking bass rules | The musical decision inside the frame |
| 4 | The timing sheet, the template, a bank of motifs | The harmony and the shape |

No other tool in the stack holds that. Canvas holds the tasks; the registered program holds the sequence; the repertoire register holds the works. Only this site can put the blueprint itself in front of a student as working material rather than a handout that went missing in Week 2, and only this site can make visible that the frame is smaller in Term 4 than it was in Term 2, which is the actual thing being assessed.

The blueprint is not a concession to weak students. It is how this teacher teaches composition and it is why the task succeeds. A site that hides the scaffolding to look more rigorous has misunderstood the course.

## Operating Context

- NSW public school, NESA **Music 7-10 Syllabus (2024)**, Stage 5 elective. Year 9 is the first half of the 200-hour course taken across Years 9 and 10.
- Four units, one per term: **Like a Version** (performing, popular music), **Blues to Jazz** (composing and performing, jazz), **Sounds of Australia** (listening, music of Australia), **Level Up: music for games and screen** (composing).
- Four assessment tasks at 25% each. AT1 performance, AT2 a two-part composition and performance, AT3 an aural paper of about 30 minutes, AT4 a composed cue with a process log and a 250-word rationale.
- Six periods a fortnight, 60-minute periods, fortnightly A/B cycle. 2027 term dates give planning figures of 28, 28, 29 and 27 periods, about 112 hours.
- **flat.io is present from Term 1 Week 1 and is used all year**, first as the bookwork and aural-notebook environment, then as the composing environment. It is a tool the site sends students to, not something the site replaces.
- Lessons slip constantly through cover, carnivals, assemblies and examination blocks. The gap between the registered sequence and the real position is the normal state.
- Daily debriefs are the update ritual: content and position changes flow from the debrief into the repo, then to the site.
- **Canvas holds assessment and only assessment**: task notifications, submission points, rubrics and SpeedGrader, and the course documents. Standing rule for every course since 11 August 2026: no teaching content on Canvas at all. The site links out to Canvas; it never duplicates it.
- The registered documents (course design, scope and sequence, four unit programs, the assessment bundle) are the authority. The site presents them and must never contradict them. To change a week, the scope and sequence changes first.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Must carry:** the registered lesson sequence for all four terms, three lessons a week across 41 teaching weeks; per-lesson learning intentions, success criteria, activities and resources; the outcome codes and content dot points each lesson delivers; the year's repertoire with embedded listening; the composing blueprints as usable material; and a visible marker of where the class is up to.

**Live position: yes**, on the Year 10 and Year 11 model, confirmed 16 August 2026. The site carries both the registered sequence and the class's real position in it, updated from debriefs.

**Adjustments are teacher-side only**, confirmed 16 August 2026. The unit programs carry a Life Skills, Low Literacy and HPGE line on every one of their 123 rows, and none of it appears on the student-facing site. One lesson page, adjustment happens live in the room, and the differentiation stays in the registered program where it is a compliance artefact. No parallel Life Skills track: no Life Skills lesson content exists on disk and none is to be invented.

**The two unseen excerpts do not go on the site**, on the Year 11 precedent. One is Term 3 Week 7's practice unseen excerpt and the other is AT3's own excerpt, sat Week 8. A student who can find either on the site in Week 6 has been handed the paper. They stay off it entirely; the site shows the taught set.

**Neither work is named anywhere in this repository**, which is public. Both are recorded in `Music7-10_Y9_RepertoireRegister.md`, off this repo, and that is the only place they are written down. Do not copy them in here to make a note easier to follow.

**The Term 3 ATSI protocol block, Weeks 4 to 6, is deliberately unfilled.** Repertoire there is the teacher's selection through the school's own consultation and the ICIP protocols. Those weeks get an honest placeholder; nothing is invented into them.

**Must never carry:** student names, student work, marks, Sentral or markbook data, NESA papers or marking guidelines, commercial audio or video files, or the repertoire register's private content (the Year 8 exclusion list is a working document, not student-facing). Audio and video are embedded from their host, never uploaded.

**Hosting:** GitHub Pages, public repository, publicly reachable with no login, `noindex` on every page. Repos live in the free `Edwards-Resources` GitHub organisation.

**School identity: none.** No school logo, letterhead or brand assets, and no school name anywhere in the repository, including internal planning files. The site is anonymous by decision, and its identity is its own.

**Unresolved and not to be assumed either way:** whether NSW Department of Education policy places conditions on staff publishing teaching material to a personal external host. Carried from the sibling sites; the teacher is content to proceed and it is not to be re-raised as a blocker.

## Brand Commitments

- Australian English throughout.
- No em dashes in anything student-facing or teacher-facing.
- **Elements of music, never "concepts of music".** That wording is 2003 and 2009 syllabus language and has already bitten the Year 10 site twice.
- **Only the ten real outcome codes exist:** MU5-PER-01/02, MU5-LIS-01/02, MU5-COM-01/02, and for Life Skills MULS-PER-01/02/03, MULS-LIS-01/02, MULS-COM-01/02/03. Outcome wording is copied verbatim from the syllabus, never paraphrased.
- **A visual world of its own.** Each year group gets its own world and the others are anti-references. **Year 8's Tour Tee is the sharpest anti-reference here**, because this cohort has just spent a year inside it, followed by Year 10's Marker Zine, Year 11's On Air, the superseded Liner Notes / Billing comps, and the `sample/` Rehearsal Marks world rejected during the Year 8 build.
- **Not cream plus a serif.** Recorded as a standing warning in the course design before the site existed. The three shipped siblings are all light paper grounds, so a fourth paper ground is the house default rather than a choice.
- **Distinctive and engaging, not a plain school course site**, carried forward from all three siblings. The structural virtues hold inside the world: unambiguous course to term to lesson hierarchy, visible position, short scannable lesson pages, generous type, accessibility as a requirement.

## Evidence on Hand

All under `Year 9 (2027)/Program and Assessment/`:

- **`Music_Y9_CourseDesign_2027.md`**: the design record, why each unit sits where it does, the notation ramp, and how the five repertoire areas are closed by Year 9 alone.
- **`Music7-10_Y9_ScopeAndSequence_2027.docx`**: all 64 Stage 5 content dot points placed, with the coverage audit rendered from the same list.
- **`Unit Programs/`, four .docx**: 123 learning-experience rows across 41 teaching weeks, three a week. Each row carries the content dot point it delivers, activities, a learning intention, success criteria, resources, an enduring understanding and three adjustment lines. Also per unit: enduring understandings, critical questions, an 18-term vocabulary table, and the school's explicit-teaching and HPGE tables.
- **The assessment bundle**: schedule, cover sheet and eight notifications (four Music 7-10, four Life Skills), all from `Tools/y9_assessment_builder.py`.
- **`Music7-10_Y9_RepertoireRegister.md`**: 26 forward picks across all four terms with the week and the use for each, plus the full record of the 47 works this cohort met in Year 8.

**What does not exist yet, and must not be assumed:**

- **No lesson content.** Unlike Year 11, which had 50 written lesson resources to pour in, Year 9 has unit-program rows only. Those rows are a real skeleton, not a scaffold, but the prose a student reads has to be authored.
- **No verified YouTube ids except two.** Only the two AT3 unseen excerpts have been through the check, and both of those are barred from the site, so neither the works nor their ids are recorded here. See the repertoire register. The other 24 picks are named works with no ids sourced. Every one needs the full order before it is written into a lesson: Apple explicitness flag, then oEmbed for a live id and the right channel, then duration.
- **No AT3 exam paper.** The notification promises about 30 minutes and four criteria at 5 marks each, and the excerpts are chosen, but the student booklet, marking guidelines and running sheet have not been built.
- **No claim about student performance or engagement**, and none is to be invented.

## Product Principles

1. **The registered program is the spine; the live position is the overlay.** Never let the second quietly rewrite the first, because the program is a compliance document.
2. **Show the frame, do not hide it.** The supplied progression, voicings, pattern and rules are the teaching, and a student must be able to get back to them without asking.
3. **A lesson page is used with hands busy**, holding an instrument or a pen. Anything needing careful reading to operate has failed.
4. **Public means public.** Every addition is judged on whether it would be safe on a billboard, because functionally it is on one.
5. **It has to survive the teacher not being in a session.** No dependency, build step or service that breaks unattended.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The site must remain fully usable with browser zoom and text scaling, meet WCAG AA contrast, and never depend on colour alone to convey position or lesson status. It has to hold on a projector in a daylit classroom and on a phone at home, which is a contrast and type-scaling requirement before it is an aesthetic one. Listening activities must not assume headphones or a particular device. The cohort includes Life Skills and low-literacy students working from the same page as everyone else, so reading level and sentence length are product constraints, not style preferences.
